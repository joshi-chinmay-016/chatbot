# Isaac Newton – Expanded Study Notes

---

## 1. Brief Biography
- **Born:** 25 December 1642 (Julian calendar) in Woolsthorpe, England.
- **Key Positions:** Lucasian Professor of Mathematics at Cambridge (1669‑1702), Warden & Master of the Royal Mint.
- **Major Works:** *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687), *Opticks* (1704).
- **Legacy:** Founder of classical mechanics, calculus (independently of Leibniz), and modern optics.

## 2. Core Scientific Principles

### 2.1 Newton’s Three Laws of Motion
| Law | Statement | Everyday Example |
|-----|-----------|-----------------|
| **First Law (Inertia)** | An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force. | A book on a table remains stationary until you push it. A hockey puck slides on ice and only stops when friction (a force) acts on it. |
| **Second Law (F = ma)** | The net force acting on an object equals the mass of the object multiplied by its acceleration ( **F = m·a** ). | Pushing a shopping cart: a light cart accelerates more than a heavily loaded one for the same push force. |
| **Third Law (Action‑Reaction)** | For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force. | When you jump, your legs exert a force on the ground, and the ground exerts an equal upward force that propels you into the air. |

#### Detailed Example – Launching a Rocket
1. **First Law:** The rocket remains at rest on the launch pad until the engines produce thrust (a net external force).
2. **Second Law:** Thrust = mass × acceleration. As fuel burns, the mass decreases, so for the same thrust the acceleration increases.
3. **Third Law:** Exhaust gases are expelled downward (action); the rocket is pushed upward (reaction).

### 2.2 Universal Law of Gravitation
- **Formula:** \[ F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \]
  - *F* – gravitational force between two masses
  - *G* – gravitational constant (6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²·kg⁻²)
  - *m₁, m₂* – masses of the objects
  - *r* – distance between the centers of the two masses
- **Key Insight:** Gravity is a **mutual** force acting at a distance; it explains both falling apples and planetary orbits.

#### Example – Weight on Earth vs. Moon
- Mass of a person: 70 kg
- Earth’s radius ≈ 6.37 × 10⁶ m, mass ≈ 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg
- Moon’s radius ≈ 1.74 × 10⁶ m, mass ≈ 7.35 × 10²² kg
- Using the formula, the weight on the Moon is about **1/6** of the weight on Earth.

### 2.3 Calculus (Fluxions)
- **Concept:** Newton developed *fluxions* (derivatives) and *fluents* (integrals) to describe changing quantities.
- **Fundamental Theorem:** The derivative of the integral of a function returns the original function, linking rates of change with accumulated quantities.

#### Example – Motion with Constant Acceleration
- Position: \( s(t) = \frac{1}{2} a t^2 + v_0 t + s_0 \)
- Velocity (first derivative): \( v(t) = a t + v_0 \)
- Acceleration (second derivative): \( a = \frac{dv}{dt} \) – constant.

---

### 2.4 Optics – Light as Particles & Waves
- **Corpuscular Theory:** Newton argued that light consists of particles (corpuscles) that travel in straight lines, explaining reflection and refraction via Snell’s law.
- **Experiments:** Split white light with a prism, showing it consists of a spectrum of colors, each with a different refractive index.
- **Legacy:** While the wave nature of light was later emphasized (Huygens, Young), Newton’s work laid the groundwork for modern optics and the later development of quantum theory (photons).

## 3. Integrated Example – Satellite Orbit
1. **Newton’s Law of Gravitation** provides the centripetal force needed for orbit: \( \frac{G M_E m}{r^2} = \frac{m v^2}{r} \).
2. Solving for orbital speed \( v \): \( v = \sqrt{\frac{G M_E}{r}} \).
3. **Second Law** confirms that with this speed, the net force (gravity) produces the required centripetal acceleration.
4. **First Law** explains that in the absence of other forces (ignoring atmospheric drag), the satellite will continue in its orbit indefinitely.
